## Overview

During a placement at **McLaren Racing**, I investigated the application of **Embedded Large Eddy Simulation (ELES)** to the aerodynamics of a Formula One front wing. The project focused on combining the efficiency of Reynolds-Averaged Navier–Stokes (RANS) simulations with the accuracy of Large Eddy Simulation (LES) to improve prediction of complex wake structures while remaining computationally practical for an industrial CFD workflow.

The work applied an embedded LES methodology to an open-source McLaren front wing geometry and compared the results against experimental Particle Image Velocimetry (PIV) measurements.

---

## The Challenge

Formula One aerodynamic development requires balancing two competing objectives:

* **High accuracy** to capture complex flow physics
* **Fast turnaround** to evaluate many design iterations within strict CFD resource limits

While RANS simulations are computationally efficient, they often struggle to accurately predict:

* Vortex development
* Wake interactions
* Three-dimensional flow separation
* Transient aerodynamic behaviour

Conversely, full-domain LES provides significantly higher fidelity but is prohibitively expensive for routine design studies. This project explored **Embedded LES (ELES)** as a compromise, using LES only where increased fidelity is most beneficial.

## Methodology

### Embedded Large Eddy Simulation (ELES)

Rather than resolving the entire computational domain with LES, a smaller **LES region** was embedded within a larger RANS simulation.

The workflow consisted of:

* Full-domain RANS simulation
* Embedded LES region downstream of the front wing
* One-way coupling from RANS to LES
* Turbulence generation at the RANS–LES interface

This approach concentrates computational effort on the downstream wake, where accurate vortex prediction is most important, while retaining the efficiency of RANS elsewhere.

The coupled simulations were performed using [**OpenFOAM**](https://www.openfoam.com/) with data exchanged between the RANS and LES solvers using the [**preCICE**](https://precice.org/) coupling library.

## Formula One Test Case

The study used an open-source geometry derived from the **McLaren MP4-17D** Formula One front wing.

Key characteristics included:

* Three-element inverted front wing
* Complex endplate geometry
* Moving ground boundary
* Reynolds number of **1.73 × 10⁵**
* Validation against published PIV experiments

Particular attention was given to the system of vortices generated by the endplate, which play a critical role in conditioning the flow around the remainder of the car.

## Turbulence Generation

A major challenge when coupling RANS and LES is generating physically realistic turbulence at the LES inlet.

This work employed an improved **Synthetic Eddy Method (SEM)** to introduce turbulent fluctuations based on the upstream RANS solution.

The implementation included:

* RANS-derived velocity and Reynolds stresses
* Synthetic turbulence generation
* Adaptive eddy placement
* Length-scale control using local turbulence quantities

## Key Results

### RANS Performance

The RANS simulation accurately predicted the vortex system immediately behind the front wing but exhibited excessive numerical dissipation further downstream, causing the vortices to decay more rapidly than observed experimentally.

### LES Performance

The LES captured downstream vortex development much more effectively, maintaining coherent vortex structures over a greater distance.

However, resolving the entire front wing using LES required substantially greater computational resources and highlighted the sensitivity of wall-resolved turbulence modelling around the wing geometry.

### Embedded LES

Initial ELES results demonstrated that:

* Stable coupling between RANS and LES was achieved.
* Synthetic turbulence successfully transitioned the embedded LES region.
* Downstream vortex development showed clear improvements over RANS.
* The strongest vortex structures were preserved further downstream without requiring a full-domain LES calculation.

## Impact

This project demonstrated how **Embedded LES** can provide a practical compromise between computational efficiency and simulation fidelity for industrial aerodynamic applications.

By resolving only the critical wake region with LES, the methodology captured complex vortex dynamics that standard RANS models failed to reproduce while avoiding the computational expense of full-domain LES. The work also demonstrated the successful integration of advanced CFD methods into an industrial Formula One simulation workflow, providing a pathway towards higher-fidelity aerodynamic analysis within the tight computational budgets of motorsport development.
